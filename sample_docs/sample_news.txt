Global Economy and Political Landscape: Key Developments in 2024

The International Monetary Fund (IMF), headquartered in Washington, D.C., revised its global growth forecast upward to 3.2% for 2024 in its World Economic Outlook published in April. Kristalina Georgieva, the Managing Director of the IMF, cited resilient consumer spending in the United States and emerging market growth as key drivers, while warning of elevated debt levels in countries like Italy, France, and Japan.

In the United States, President Joe Biden signed the CHIPS and Science Act into law at the White House in August 2022, committing $52.7 billion to boost domestic semiconductor manufacturing. Intel Corporation announced plans to build two new factories in Ohio, while Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) began construction of its second fabrication plant in Phoenix, Arizona, backed by a $6.6 billion federal grant announced in April 2024.

The United Kingdom held a general election on July 4, 2024, resulting in a landslide victory for the Labour Party led by Keir Starmer. The Conservative Party, which had governed Britain since 2010 under a succession of prime ministers including Boris Johnson, Liz Truss, and Rishi Sunak, suffered its worst electoral defeat in a century. Starmer was formally appointed Prime Minister at Buckingham Palace in London by King Charles III.

In Germany, Chancellor Olaf Scholz faced a political crisis when his three-party coalition collapsed in November 2024 after Finance Minister Christian Lindner of the Free Democratic Party (FDP) was dismissed. Scholz announced early elections would be held in February 2025, bringing uncertainty to Europe's largest economy amid ongoing challenges from the war in Ukraine and sluggish industrial output.

The war in Ukraine, which began with Russia's full-scale invasion on February 24, 2022, continued into its third year. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky addressed the United Nations General Assembly in New York City in September 2024, appealing for continued military and financial support. The NATO alliance, meeting at its summit in Washington, D.C. in July 2024, reaffirmed its commitment to Ukraine's defense, with Secretary-General Jens Stoltenberg announcing a $43 billion annual support package.

Brazil held its G20 presidency in 2024, with President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva hosting world leaders in Rio de Janeiro in November. The summit focused on climate change, global hunger, and international taxation of ultra-high-net-worth individuals — a proposal championed by French economist Gabriel Zucman. The World Bank and the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) expressed support for a minimum 2% wealth tax on billionaires.

In the Middle East, tensions remained elevated throughout 2024. The conflict in Gaza, which escalated following the Hamas attack on Israel on October 7, 2023, dominated diplomatic discussions at the United Nations Security Council in New York. US Secretary of State Antony Blinken made multiple trips to Doha, Qatar and Cairo, Egypt to mediate ceasefire negotiations. The International Court of Justice (ICJ) in The Hague, Netherlands issued a preliminary ruling in January 2024 ordering Israel to prevent acts of genocide.

India surpassed Japan to become the world's fourth-largest economy in 2024, according to data from the World Bank. Prime Minister Narendra Modi secured a third consecutive term in elections held in April and May, though his Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) lost its outright parliamentary majority and had to rely on coalition partners. India's economic growth of 8.2% in fiscal year 2024 was the fastest among major economies.

Amazon.com, Inc. announced in September 2024 that it would invest $110 billion in cloud infrastructure over the next five years across data centers in Virginia, Texas, Ohio, Ireland, and Singapore. Amazon Web Services CEO Adam Selipsky presented the investment plan at Amazon's annual re:Invent conference in Las Vegas, Nevada. The company also committed $4 billion to Anthropic, the AI safety company founded by Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei, formerly of OpenAI.

The Federal Reserve, led by Chair Jerome Powell, began cutting interest rates in September 2024 — the first reduction since the aggressive hiking cycle that began in March 2022 to combat inflation. The European Central Bank in Frankfurt, Germany, under President Christine Lagarde, had already begun cutting rates in June 2024. The Bank of England in London maintained a cautious approach amid persistent wage growth in the British economy.
